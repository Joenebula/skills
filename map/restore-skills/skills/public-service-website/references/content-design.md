## Reading age 9

Aim for a reading age of 9 (core principle 3). This is not dumbing down — it is respecting that people read public service content while stressed, distracted, in a second language, or in a hurry. Even expert readers prefer plain content.

Practical targets: short sentences (aim ~15–20 words, vary for rhythm), one idea per sentence, common everyday words, active voice.

## Voice and tone

- **Voice** is constant: clear, calm, honest, respectful, never patronising.
- **Tone** flexes with context: reassuring when someone is anxious (reporting a problem, bad news), brisk and efficient for routine tasks, warm but not flippant.
- Address the user as "you". Refer to the organisation as "we". This is direct and human.
- No corporate or political throat-clearing. Lead with what the user can do.

## Plain English techniques

- Prefer common words: "buy" not "purchase", "help" not "assistance", "about" not "regarding", "use" not "utilise".
- Cut filler: "in order to" → "to", "at this moment in time" → "now".
- Active voice: "We'll email you" not "An email will be sent".
- Expand or avoid acronyms; define any unavoidable jargon on first use.
- Use specific numbers and dates, not vague ones ("within 5 working days" not "shortly").
- Front-load: put the most important information first, in the first sentence and the first paragraph.

## Structure for scanning

People scan public service pages; they rarely read top to bottom.

- Meaningful headings and subheadings that work as a table of contents.
- Short paragraphs (2–3 sentences).
- Bulleted lists for sets of items or options; numbered lists for sequences.
- Bold sparingly, for genuinely key terms — not whole sentences.
- One main task or message per page.

## Microcopy

- **Buttons** describe the action and outcome: "Report the fault", not "Submit". The label is also the accessible name (SC 2.5.3).
- **Links** make sense out of context: "Read the council tax guide", not "click here".
- **Error messages** say what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain words, no blame: "Enter your postcode" not "Invalid input in field 3".
- **Form hints** appear before the field and explain the format expected.
- **Empty states and confirmations** tell the user what happened and what's next, with any reference number.

## Inclusive language

- People-first, respectful terms; follow current disability and equality language guidance.
- Don't assume gender, family structure, housing tenure, immigration status, or digital access.
- Avoid idioms and cultural references that don't translate.
- Write for the diversity of the actual public, not an imagined default user.

## Readability checking

- Read it aloud — if you stumble, rewrite.
- Run a readability score (Flesch–Kincaid grade ~5–6, or a Hemingway-style check) as a rough gauge, not a target to game.
- Test labels and navigation wording with real users (tree testing — see `references/ux-research-and-ia.md`).
- Honour **SC 3.1.5 Reading Level** (AAA) as an aspiration: where complex content is unavoidable, provide a plain-language summary.
